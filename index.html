01PM · ONGOING

Magic Academy

PM + backend-leaning full stack · team of 4

A multi-agent society simulator set in a magic university, where student and professor agents build memory, relationships, and reputation that emerge into full relationships, organizations, and events. Every piece of magic has to change something mechanical — access, agent state, trust, or what happens next — not just reskin an ordinary task. Selected from competing team proposals at a WISET hackerspace mentoring program.

ORIGIN

Wrote a proposal for this project at a WISET hackerspace mentoring program, competing against other teams' pitches — it was selected, and I became PM. Also drove the initial branding and UI mockups myself before any code was written.

SYSTEM DECISION

Split the system into six one-directional layers — Tick Engine, Event Master Agent, Agent Runtime, Policy Engine, Commit Service — so every write to the database goes through one place.

SECURITY DECISION

The Event Master Agent treats an agent's name and personality fields as untrusted input, not instructions — the system prompt locks its role so injected text can't redirect it. It's also granted no permissions beyond the LLM call itself; all DB reads and writes stay with the Orchestrator, and two consecutive parse failures escalate to a human instead of guessing.

LEADERSHIP

Redesigned how the team collaborates with AI itself — role personas cut from 8 to 3, repeatable workflows turned into skills, a three-question approval gate for anything irreversible.

06FEB 2026 · SOLO

Video Codec Analysis Pipeline

HEVC / VVC compression pipeline

A five-stage pipeline takes raw video through analysis, parallel encoding, metric calculation, and result visualization — comparing HEVC against next-gen VVC on compression efficiency (BD-Rate) and quality (PSNR) end to end.

SYSTEM DECISION

Threads can't truly parallelize CPU-bound encoding past Python's GIL, so I used ProcessPoolExecutor to spawn separate OS-level processes instead, letting the OS scheduler distribute jobs across CPU cores, then processed completed jobs asynchronously as they finished to cut idle time.

DATA DECISION

Profiled each clip's data characteristics first, then used that profile to set compression priority instead of applying uniform settings across the board.

SCOPE DECISIONSelf-upgraded

The comparison itself only needed BD-Rate and PSNR numbers. I added a fifth stage — automated result visualization — so the trade-off could be read at a glance instead of parsed from raw logs.

[ 05 — LEADERSHIP ]

Structure, for people.

01

Design the structure.

I'm the type who needs a plan before starting anything, and I like doing things fast and thorough at once — systematically. Leading meant scaling that instinct across the whole team. So I started by redrawing the boundaries themselves — role personas cut from 8 to 3, repeatable workflows turned into skills, and a three-question approval gate for anything irreversible.

02

Understand the people.

Structure alone wasn't enough. I learned to design for different motivations and skill levels on the team, not just for the workflow. I try to let people work in the areas they actually want to grow into, and split roles so the team grows together — not just around what needs to get done.

03

Structure for people.

I design structure so people can work comfortably, not the other way around. Watching how the team actually worked, and rebuilding role personas around activities instead of owners, changed how I think about leading. Rebuilding Claude Teams from 8 owner-based personas into 3 activity-based ones is that shift, in practice.
